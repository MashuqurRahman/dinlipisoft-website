Is Your HR Team Spending Too Much Time Updating Excel Sheets?

Aug 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Excel is one of the most widely used tools in business.

It is flexible, familiar, and useful for many tasks.

But when an entire HR department depends on multiple spreadsheets for employee management, attendance, leave, overtime, and payroll, problems can start to appear.

The issue is not Excel itself.

The issue is using a general-purpose spreadsheet as the primary system for increasingly complex HR operations.

The Spreadsheet Dependency Problem

Imagine having one spreadsheet for employee records, another for attendance, another for leave, another for overtime, and another for payroll.

Now imagine updating information across all of them every month.

The same employee information may need to be entered or modified multiple times.

This creates repetitive work and increases the chance of inconsistencies.

More Employees Means More Complexity

A manual HR process may work reasonably well when a company has a small team.

But as employee numbers grow, the amount of data grows with them.

More employees mean:

  • More attendance records
  • More leave requests
  • More overtime calculations
  • More employee information
  • More payroll data
  • More reporting requirements

At some point, HR teams need more than spreadsheets.

HR Should Spend Less Time on Repetitive Work

HR professionals have responsibilities that go far beyond data entry.

They need to support employees, manage organizational policies, coordinate recruitment, improve employee experience, and provide useful information to management.

If a significant amount of their time is spent updating spreadsheets, those higher-value activities can receive less attention.
